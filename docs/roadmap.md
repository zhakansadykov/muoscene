# Roadmap

Revised on 21 November 2026, after roadmap task 0.1-08: the order of the remaining tasks of 0.1 and the content of the later versions follow from who the program is for.

## Who it is for

muoscene is a program to be installed and used, on Windows, macOS and Linux, by a group that exposes and scans muography detectors — nuclear emulsion first, straw telescopes later. Most of its users do not write code. What such a user does: takes a scene, ready-made or generated, of the ground and the structures in it; puts detectors into it by coordinates and direction; gets the expected maps; loads the tracks of the scanned detectors; sees data against model in figures and numbers. The author of the processing program of the group needs the file formats written down and stable. Everything else in this roadmap serves that.

Two rules follow.

- Every version ends with something such a user can do without opening the code, and says what.
- Accuracy is spent where it changes a result. Until scattering and a measured energy response of the detector are in the maps — scattering moves 8 to 26 % of the signal of the reference void out of its cone, and a threshold of 1 GeV instead of 0.2 GeV takes its significance from 11.5σ to 9.4σ (`docs/numbers.md`) — no task is spent on an effect below 0.2 %.

One task is one working session and one or a few commits. A task is done when it has code, tests, docstrings, a `CHANGELOG.md` line and its box ticked here.

Architecture (decided 13 November 2026, `docs/notes/mulder.md`): geometry navigation and muon physics come from Calzone and Mulder; muoscene adds the scene description, a fast flux path through tables built by Mulder, detector responses, planning, and comparison with data.

## 0.1 — scenes, flux tables, parity

Goal: a user installs muoscene with one command on Windows, macOS or Linux, runs `muoscene plan examples/canyon_demo/run.yaml` and gets a run folder with the expected maps, figures and a report for a synthetic canyon with a pier at the site altitude; moving a detector is an edit of one line of `detectors.yaml`. The report holds the significance, and in legacy mode the preliminary sensitivity calculation is reproduced. The reference at the site altitude and the rendered 3D scene follow in 0.1.1. Conventions — `docs/conventions.md`.

- [x] **0.1-02 Prior-art spike: Mulder and Calzone.** Thirteen experiments and the decision: hybrid on top of Mulder and Calzone. `docs/notes/mulder.md`, `docs/notes/spike/mulder_spike.py`.
- [x] **0.1-01 Conventions.** Approved on 15 November 2026: kinetic energy at the detector, default threshold 1 GeV, reference at the site altitude in 0.1, runs outside the domain of validity refused.
- [x] **0.1-03 Reference numbers.** The preliminary scripts ported to `tests/regression/legacy.py` (bit-identical to the originals) and frozen on a synthetic canyon in `legacy_reference.json` (55 cases, 10.31σ for the reference void); the spike frozen in `mulder_reference.json` (57 cases at a relative tolerance of 10⁻⁵, above the spread between Linux and macOS, including the tables behind discrete transport and the wall paths of the synthetic canyon); `docs/legacy.md` with the sizes of the simplifications. Found on the way: integrals over all energies across the altitude cut-off were wrong by up to 1.3 % in the spike (season and geomagnetic numbers corrected in `docs/notes/mulder.md`); Mulder gives the air of a local geometry sea-level density: 1000 m of it above the brow lowers the counts of the reference void at 1150 m by 1 % (0.1-06: air at the site density, or a thin air layer).
- [x] **0.1-04 Materials.** `muoscene.materials`: materials from formulas, element fractions or oxide analyses with an explicit mean excitation energy (tabulated, or the Berger–Seltzer rule); mixtures; soils from dry density and gravimetric water content as a mixture of skeleton and water, so that Mulder takes the density effect at the particle density; names in Mulder derived from the definition; CSDA ranges, energy to cross, weights w(K) and equivalent opacity. Library with sources: water, standard rock, concrete, air, quartz, calcite, silicate and loess skeletons. Mulder ranges agree with the PDG tables within 0.52 %; the spike thresholds are reproduced exactly; concrete weighs 0.9952 against the spike loam at 7 GeV; moisture round trip to 1e-13. Sizes of the choices: `docs/notes/materials.md`.
- [x] **Review of 16 November 2026.** Independent check of the numbers, tests and documents. Every significance now states its detector threshold: the reference void is frozen above 0.2 and 1 GeV as well (11.52σ and 9.37σ at 1150 m against 12.05σ with every muon counted), 63 frozen cases; the scattering share is a mean over three seeds (0.74 ± 0.02 without a threshold, 0.92 ± 0.02 above 1 GeV) and has a validation test of the discrete mode; `docs/numbers.md` holds the headline numbers with their conditions. Found: Mulder applies decay along the air path; the cut-off survives under soil thinner than about 130 g/cm² (frozen as `thin_slab`); plain sums from a threshold are 2–3 % high. Materials refuse a repeated element and values outside the tables; the private-data guard allows a short list of file types instead of refusing a list; CI pins the backends and a weekly workflow meets their new releases; the pier of the spike is invented. Conventions: revision 3.
- [x] **0.1-05 Flux tables.** `muoscene.flux`. `FluxTable` gives the differential flux against kinetic energy, sine of elevation and opacity and its integral over a detector energy response (`EnergyResponse`, always given explicitly) by trapezoids that start at the threshold; it holds the open-sky flux at the detector altitude on a grid of energy and sine of elevation and takes the opacity through the CSDA range and stopping power of the reference material, which is what continuous transport does (conventions, revision 4). `MulderTable` builds it with Mulder — atmosphere model, optional date and azimuth for the geomagnetic field, reference chosen per conventions (GCCLY15 at sea level in 0.1.0; a tabulated reference is refused until 0.1-05b) — finds, stores and enforces the altitude cut-off, compares every new table with Mulder under a dense slab and caches it in the user cache directory by a hash of its inputs. `LegacyTable` repeats the arithmetic of the preliminary calculation to the last bit. Met: 60.50 m⁻² s⁻¹ sr⁻¹ above 1 GeV at sea level; the cut-offs of the spike and the frozen `slab` and `thin_slab` cases; within 0.045 % of Mulder under a slab at 16 150 random points up to 1 TeV, integrals above 0.2 and 1 GeV within 6·10⁻⁵; a site at −430 m has 56.69 m⁻² s⁻¹ sr⁻¹ and no cut-off; more than 3·10⁴ g/cm², energies below the cut-off and values outside the grid are refused; a sum that gives its first node a whole step is 2.9–3.3 % high by the vertical. Found: above 1 TeV the flux of Mulder under a thick slab wavers by 0.3 %; a table at the altitude of a detector under 10–30 m of soil is 0.05–0.10 % low, the air it has in place of that soil. 70 frozen cases. `docs/notes/flux.md`, `docs/numbers.md`.
- [ ] **0.1-05b Reference at the site altitude (0.1.1, after the release of 0.1.0).** Open-sky differential flux of muons in K and elevation for a given altitude, latitude and date from a model of the atmospheric flux: PARMA4 / EXPACS (T. Sato, PLoS ONE 10 (2015) e0144679 and 11 (2016) e0160390), which also gives electrons and positrons, with MCEq (optional `mceq` extra) as a cross-check above 1 GeV; tables cached and shipped for a grid of altitudes so that the core runs without either; passed to Mulder as a tabulated reference; the soft component as a table of its own for the track-density budget; normalisation against GCCLY15 at sea level; altitude dependence checked against published measurements at mountain altitudes (sources chosen in the task and listed in `docs/notes/reference.md`); a measured open-sky spectrum can replace the table. Whether PARMA holds at the zenith angles and energies needed, and where MCEq stops being valid at low energy, is established first. *Done when:* at sea level the table agrees with GCCLY15 within its stated uncertainty above 1 GeV; at 1150 m and 3340 m there is no cut-off and the flux above 1 GeV agrees with the sea-level reference carried up by Mulder, which already contains decay, within the size of the production effect and of the difference between the models, both quantified; the track density of open sky is given with and without the soft component; the choice of reference follows the conventions automatically.
- [x] **0.1-06 Geometry.** `muoscene.geometry`. A scene is a list of bodies in the world frame: `Ground` — a profile of the surface in the x–z plane extruded along y, cut by horizontal `Layer`s; `Structure` — boxes and vertical cylinders, `pile_grid`; `Cut` — a niche; `Anomaly` — a sphere that scales the density of its host. The builder makes the Calzone tree: one mesh for every connected piece of ground between two layer tops, the pieces side by side; cuts subtracted from the pieces they reach; structures split at layer tops and placed as daughters of the ground around them; anomalies as daughters of their host; centimetres and positions relative to mothers. The air of a scene — its box and its cuts — has the density of the atmosphere model at the site altitude, and the box is 1 m larger than the bodies. `Scene.scan` gives a `PathTable`, the path length per body and direction, and the table gives the opacity under a hypothesis — bulk densities of bodies, relative changes of anomalies — without a new scan. Refused: bodies that overlap, a cut through a structure, a structure across the surface, an anomaly across any boundary, a detector within 1 µm of a boundary or outside the scene, directions that are not unit vectors towards the sky, lines of sight cut by the limits of the model. Met: the frozen wall-with-niche paths within 1.1·10⁻¹³ m and layers adding up to the ground to the same; chords of the box, the cylinders and the sphere of the invented pier within 3·10⁻¹² m; 4.0·10⁵ and 2.7·10⁵ directions/s with 14 and 34 media on the Linux VM, 5.9·10⁴ and 5.4·10⁴ on macOS on the same machine, 8 % above the criterion: the speed test is marked `validation`. Found: 1000 m of scene air above the brow lower the counts of the reference void above 1 GeV by 0.91 % at Mulder's default density and still by 0.31 % at the density of the site, so the box is thin as well; Mulder crashes on a scan from outside its geometry, never returns from a nan direction, divides lengths by the length of a direction and reads a zero density as that of the material; the `bvh` mesh traversal of Calzone loses path where two meshes touch. No rotations: a scene is described along its own axes and the frame is turned in 0.1-07. 76 frozen cases. `docs/notes/geometry.md`, `docs/numbers.md`.
- [x] **0.1-07 Site and detectors I.** `muoscene.site` and `muoscene.detectors`. `Site` holds the origin of the world frame and the azimuth of the +y axis of a scene from true north, and `Site.scan` is the only place where points and directions pass between ENU and the axes of a scene; `Scene` takes that azimuth for the local frame of Mulder alone, which needs it for the geomagnetic field. `Pose` per conventions, with axes, slopes, the sky side of a line and the way back from axes to angles. `DirectionGrid`: rectangular bins in the slopes with exact solid angles, also for the part of a bin within the acceptance circle (a contour integral in closed form), sub-samples whose weights add up to it, a default grid of 40 × 40 bins of 0.05 with 4 × 4 sub-samples. `Detector`: accepted circle, energy response (always given), efficiency, sensitive area against the slopes — A for a film, the overlap of two planes for a telescope. `EmulsionStack` with `track_density`: a sum over both sides of a film that carries its threshold, exposure and field of view. A detector takes a flux table and the opacity along its lines of sight and calls the table with its own response; the source of the opacity is 0.1-08. Met: bin solid angles add up to the cone within 2·10⁻¹⁵ (10⁻³ to 10⁻⁵ if edge bins were taken by the share of sub-samples); pose round trips to 6·10⁻¹⁴ degrees and 3·10⁻¹⁶; azimuth and signs on paths worked out by hand and against the local frame of Mulder; the frozen track densities of the preliminary calculation within 0.03 % for horizontal and 0.10 % for tilted films at a Monte Carlo error of 0.21–0.39 %, to 4·10⁻¹⁶ on its own directions; against Mulder through the scene within 0.06 %. At 1150 m a horizontal film in a niche 12 and 22 m below a flat brow collects 34 758 and 30 346 muon tracks per cm² above 1 GeV in 60 days from elevations above 16°, 76 and 86 % of them from the side of the canyon; above 0.2 GeV only the side of the ground is defined, 10 081 and 4794, and the whole is refused until 0.1-05b. Significances come with 0.1-09 and are quoted for both thresholds there. Found: with the frame of a scene left unturned Mulder takes its y axis for north, 0.25 % between east and west with the field of a date; the preliminary calculation counts both sides of a tilted film, 0.5 % of the density at 20° and 9.4 % at 45°, which the acceptance of the conventions leaves out; the azimuth of a scene and the two sides of a film are not in the conventions yet (proposed for revision 5). 78 frozen cases. `docs/notes/detectors.md`, `docs/numbers.md`.
- [x] **0.1-08 Forward model.** `muoscene.model` and `muoscene.transport`. `expected_map` gives an `ExpectedMap`, an `xarray.Dataset` over detector, hypothesis and the bins of a grid, for `Station`s — a detector, its pose, the flux table of its altitude, its exposure — and a `HypothesisSet`: no anomaly, a void, a decompaction, other bulk densities, all sums over one scan. The equivalent opacity of a path through several materials takes its weights between the first energy of the detector response and the energy needed above the path, by iteration, so that no energy and no order of the materials is assumed; the table stays at the altitude of the detector and the air the solid bodies displace is taken out of the opacity. `Detector.counts` sums flux × projected area × efficiency × solid angle × time over the accepted lines of a bin; `Site.differential_flux` is Mulder through the scene in ENU, and every run compares the fast path with it on 256 random lines of sight per detector, stores the deviation with the seed and refuses above 0.5 %. Lines of sight a table has no flux for are refused or, on request, marked bin by bin with the reason, never filled. Met on the invented pier of the spike at 1150 m: rms 0.014 % and at most 0.033 % above 1 GeV, at most 0.061 % above 0.2 GeV; within 0.05 % of a pier built without muoscene; a uniform slab within 0.021 % of Mulder and the open sky within 0.005 %; a hypothesis without a new scan equal to a rebuilt scene to 10⁻¹². Above 0.2 GeV 356 of the 1324 bins of a horizontal film in the niche have no flux with the sea-level reference, and the open sky none (0.1-05b). Proposed for the next revision of the conventions: the weight with a threshold, the displaced air, the mask of bins without a flux. 80 frozen cases. `docs/notes/forward.md`, `docs/numbers.md`.
- [x] **0.1-10 Examples.** `examples/canyon_demo`, `examples/open_sky`, `examples/slab`: each a folder a user copies and runs with `python run.py`, its parameters at the top of the script and the detectors a table of one line each; `site.yaml`, `detectors.yaml` and `run.yaml` come with the schema in 0.1-11, which keeps the numbers of the tests of the examples (decided on 22 November 2026). `muoscene.geometry.canyon` generates the canyon: a trough of two banks with different brows in one `Ground`, horizontal layers through both, a pier on each bank with a pile cap level with the surface and 3 × 3 piles. A detector stands in a niche cut into the wall where it is or, without a niche, in front of the wall as on pins (decided on 22 November 2026: a niche may be too slow and costly to cut), and is placed by bank, distance along the canyon and depth below the brow, so that moving it or changing its mount is one edit of its line. The demo has six films — two pairs of a niche and pins side by side, a niche and pins on the other bank — a void and a decompaction zone, and prints, for 1 and 0.2 GeV, the bins with a number and why the others have none, the tracks, what each anomaly adds and where, each pair side by side and the check against Mulder; `open_sky` and `slab` check known answers and end with the number of checks passed, the first check of an installation. Met: above 1 GeV every bin of every film has a number; on the wall a film collects 20 % more tracks than in a niche, a part of its cone being open sky, and the void changes its bins by about as much, +12.1 against +12.6 %; above 0.2 GeV the films on the wall have 942 and 622 bins with a number against 1292 and 990 in the niches; the open sky equals the flux table to 7·10⁻¹⁶; under a slab the opacity is ρ d / cos θ to 8·10⁻¹⁶ and, with the loam of the frozen cases, the flux along the vertical and 45° is that of `threshold_slab` within 0.019 %. Found: the check of the fast path integrated the flux of Mulder over energy too coarsely where a threshold is just above the cut-off of the reference and refused right runs, 0.62 % at 15.5° of elevation above 1 GeV at 1150 m; the direct flux has 240 energy nodes a decade over the first half decade from the threshold since, 0.02 % there, and on the pier of 0.1-08 the largest deviation above 0.2 GeV is 0.049 % instead of 0.061 %. A film tilted into the massif by 44° or more is refused: its lowest lines run along the wall out of the model. `docs/numbers.md`, section Examples. *Done when:* the parameters are documented and differ from any real site.

## 0.2 — real sites

Coordinate systems with pyproj; true north, meridian convergence, magnetic declination from IGRF; mesh bodies (OBJ and STL with face normals) and DEM to a closed mesh through Calzone; survey import (LAS/LAZ, CSV points, LandXML, DXF, GeoTIFF) and point cloud to a closed mesh; layer horizons as nested height-field solids; structures crossing non-planar horizons; voxel overlay and ray-by-voxel path matrix; template of a private site workspace outside the repository.

For the user: a scene is a folder of files — the terrain or the wall as a mesh or a survey, the layers, the structures — and `muoscene check` draws what it read; the detectors of a run are a table (CSV or a YAML list) of name, type, coordinates in the survey system or in the local frame, azimuth and tilt of the normal, and exposure dates, filled in without Python.

## 0.3 — physics, planning and the workbench

Seasonal atmospheres and seasonal site-altitude references; geomagnetic field as a systematic of the open-sky reference; mixed-mode tables for opacity beyond 3e4 g/cm²; scattering — discrete-mode maps for final numbers and a kernel calibrated on them for planning loops; angular efficiency tables; latent-image fading from temperature and humidity logs; track-density budget including transport and storage; systematics framework and profile-likelihood significance; sensitivity volume per voxel; optimisation of niches and tilts under film and scanning budgets; required accuracy of orientation and wall survey; report on how the full physics changes the preliminary numbers.

Workbench. A local application on top of the same Python core for a user who does not write code: open a scene and see it in 3D; add a detector by picking a point of a wall or typing coordinates, and set its direction; start a run and look at the maps and the report. It reads and writes the configuration files of the command line, so a run made in the window is a run anyone can repeat. The technology is chosen in a spike at the start of the version, as Mulder was; a local web application is the first candidate, and the core is not rewritten for it.

## 0.4 — data

`TrackSet` in `detectors.tracks`; a reader for the output of the processing program of the scanning group, in a format fixed with its author before the task starts and written down in `docs/formats.md`; a reader for FEDRA ROOT; the scan-frame transform; binning with the same grid and cuts as the model; data/model ratio, pulls, χ², region fits with nuisance parameters; synthetic tracks in the scanning output format, for blind tests of the chain; first comparison with an archival emulsion exposure. Until scattering is in the maps every comparison states the scattering share of its configuration, measured with the discrete mode of Mulder.

In the workbench: load scanned tracks and see data against model. Straw telescope (level-1 response) and generic two-plane telescope, with a reader for their data.

The numbers of these two versions are kept because code and documents refer to them (scattering and systematics: 0.3; tracks and telescopes: 0.4). Their order is not fixed by them: if the format of the scanning output and an archival exposure are at hand before the summer of 2027, the data part is done first.

## 1.0 — release and paper

Validation: analytic cases, the preliminary calculation, continuous against discrete transport, MUTE for intensities at depth, an archival exposure; documentation (guide, theory, configuration reference generated from the schema, API); one script per paper figure; Zenodo DOI; PyPI; software paper.

For the user: installation without setting up a Python environment — a packaged application for Windows and macOS — and a guide in English and Russian that starts from a scene and ends at data against model.

## 2.0 — inversion

Joint regularised inversion on the voxel grid with borehole priors; comparison with real exposure data.
